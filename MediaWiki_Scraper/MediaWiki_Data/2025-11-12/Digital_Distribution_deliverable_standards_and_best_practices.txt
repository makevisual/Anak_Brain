When it comes to digital distribution deliverable standards and best practices, there are several key considerations to keep in mind. These practices help ensure that digital content is delivered efficiently, accurately, and in a format that is compatible with various platforms and devices. Here are some standards and best practices to follow:

# '''File Formats''': Use widely supported file formats to ensure compatibility across different devices and platforms. Common file formats include MP3 for audio, MP4 for video screeners, ProResHQ 422 for video deliverables, and PDF for documents. However, it's always important to check specific requirements and guidelines for the intended distribution platform.
# '''Metadata''': Include accurate and comprehensive metadata with your digital deliverables. Metadata provides information about the content, such as title, artist, album, description, and genre. This information helps with searchability, categorization, and organization of digital content.
# '''Quality Control''': Perform quality control checks on your deliverables before distribution. Ensure that files are free from errors, such as glitches, distortion, or corruption. Check for any missing or incomplete content and address any issues before distribution.
# '''Compression and Encoding''': Optimize file sizes through compression and encoding techniques without compromising the quality of the content. This helps reduce download times and storage requirements. Use industry-standard codecs and compression algorithms to ensure compatibility across platforms. We have some encoder specs for the best compression on our server located: U:\resources\Presets\Adobe Media Encoder
# '''File Naming Conventions''': Use consistent and descriptive file naming conventions. This makes it easier to organize and locate files and reduces confusion when working with multiple deliverables. Include relevant information such as the title, version number, and file format extension in the file name. Please note that some clients have a specific naming convention for deliverables but most the time it is a naming convention for the location it is being delivered to, i.e. Extreme Reach (ER) only allows for the ISCI to be the filename.
#* Example Slated Deliverable with full name - TVC_2023_Fall_Hot_Wheels_Hot_Wheels_City_Ultimate_Garage_30s_MAHZ3087000H
#* Example Slated Deliverable just ISCI for ER - MAHZ3087000H
# '''Version Control''': Maintain a clear version control system for your deliverables. This ensures that the latest version of your content is being distributed and prevents confusion or mix-ups with older versions. Consider using version control tools or systems to track changes and manage revisions. At MAKE we handle this in both our project saves and our exports, always check that the latest version number is the correct one that was sent.
# '''Compliance with Platform Guidelines''': Familiarize yourself with the specific guidelines and requirements of the platforms or distribution channels you intend to use. Different platforms may have their own specifications regarding file formats, metadata, artwork, and other aspects of digital content. Adhering to these guidelines increases the chances of successful distribution and reduces the risk of content rejection. As previously stated, some delivery places like Extreme Reach have specific delivery specifications to upload to them, always reach out before the delivery day to verify what the guidelines are and if they have changed from a previous delivery.
# '''Backup and Redundancy''': Maintain backups of your deliverables to protect against data loss. Implement appropriate backup strategies, both on-site and off-site, to ensure that your content is safe and can be restored if needed.

Remember that digital distribution practices and standards can evolve over time, so it's important to stay updated with the latest industry developments and platform requirements. If in doubt, a simple request to the client for deliverable verification often answers all the questions.
