== Definition of 'Styleframe' ==

* Styleframes are crucial materials acting as proof of concept in production. They provide both clients and artists overall visual, creative directions of a project and demonstrate the design, mood, style, etc
* Following is a short article about the definition of styleframe(s), which seems to be a good, simple explanation.
** [https://schwittek.com/teaching/resources/what-is-a-styleframe What is a styleframe?] 

== Technical approaches to make styleframes ==

* At MAKE, depending on the amount of time to spend on, subjects of designs, design components, or an artist's preference, there are two major software to use to create styleframes.

=== Photoshop ===

* Using Photoshop involves lots of photocollage, which means searching for good source images is important.
* With Photoshop, we may create multiple 'frames' of styleframes to demonstrate the overall idea of a motion, like a small section of storyboards.
* We often make a full 'styleboard' (storyboard that is made with styleframes) if we pitch simple designs such as minimal motion graphics or minor graphic components.

=== After Effect ===

* After Effect can be used to catch two birds with one stone; both motion tests and styleframes.
* If we pitch simple designs like motion graphics or graphic components, sometimes motion tests are considered more effective than static styleframes. Either way, it is a way to generate styleframes and more :)
* We also work with 3D artists to make styleframes as well. They can provide designers 3D renders of products or main objects for styleframes.

==== 2. HD vs. Social ====

* Many projects nowadays goes live not only on TV, but also on social media. Naturally clients ask for final deliveries in many different ratios, including..
** HD/16x9 for TV or Youtube/Hulu/other streaming platforms
** 1x1, 4x5, 9x16 for general SNS platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Twitter, etc
* Designing styleframes that work in different ratios makes the overall production more efficient as it allows artists to plan ahead of the production.

==== 3. Take some time to brainstorm and sketch out your idea before jumping into making styleframes right away. ====

==== 4. Pay attention to the creative brief: staying on the directions and creative needs is very important. Taking notes is one way to remind yourself of those. ====

== Styleframe Spec ==

==== 1. It depends on a final delivery specification. Clients often ask for social versions of spots, so be mindful when you set up a comp for styleframes. ====

* If final deliveries only require HD 16x9, we do in HD/16x9 ratio - JPEG or PNG, 1920x1080, 8 bit RGB, 72 dpi
* If final deliveries require HD 16x9 AND SNS formats, we work on 1x1 ratio - JPEG or PNG, 1920x1920 or 3840 x 3840, 8 bit RGB, 72 dpi
** This can be cropped in different ratios if necessary.

==== 2. Naming convention ====

* Default

 [Project Initial]_Styleframe__[Spot title]_S010__F010_v001

* Multiple variations

 [Project Initial]_Styleframe__[Spot title]__[Variation ex: A,B,C..]_S010__F010_v001

* Multiple artists and variations

 [Project Initial]_Styleframe__[Spot title]__[Artist's name]_[Variation ex: A,B,C...]_S010__F010_v001
